# TastyBites Product Pricing & Profitability Analysis | Power BI

## Project Overview

This project analyzes product pricing, sales performance, discount impact, and profitability for **TastyBites**, using Power BI to translate transactional sales data into actionable business recommendations.

The analysis covers **3,000 orders, 20 products, 4 product categories, and 2 years of transaction data (October 2024 – September 2026)**.

The primary objective is to identify revenue and profit drivers, understand the relationship between discounting and profitability, identify potential price-adjustment opportunities, and recommend promotional and bundling strategies that could improve overall profitability.

## Business Objectives

- Identify products and categories generating the highest revenue and profit.
- Evaluate the financial impact of discounts on products and categories.
- Identify high-volume products that contribute relatively less profit.
- Shortlist products for controlled price-adjustment experiments.
- Recommend targeted promotions and bundling strategies.
- Develop a KPI-driven view of product pricing and profitability.

### Data Cleaning & Preparation

- Standardized inconsistent product category labels.
- Replaced missing discount values with 0%, following the case study's agreed assumption that a missing discount represents no discount.
- Verified data types for numerical, date, and categorical columns.
- Prepared calculated measures for revenue, cost, profit, margin, discount impact, and order value.

**Data assumption:** Treating a missing discount as 0% is an analytical assumption, not proof that no discount was applied. This assumption should be validated against the original business records in a real-world deployment.

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

### 1. Revenue per Product

Measures net sales revenue generated by each product after discounts.

**Formula:**

`Net Revenue = Quantity × Unit Price × (1 − Discount % / 100)`

### 2. Profit Margin per Product

Measures the percentage of net revenue retained as profit after product costs.

**Formula:**

`Profit Margin % = Total Profit / Net Revenue × 100`

### 3. Units Sold

Measures total quantities sold for each product.

**Formula:**

`Units Sold = SUM(Quantity)`

### 4. Discount Impact

Measures the monetary value of discounts applied to sales.

**Formula:**

`Discount Impact = Quantity × Unit Price × Discount % / 100`

This represents revenue sacrificed through discounts; it is not automatically equal to net profit lost because discounting may also influence sales volume.

### 5. Contribution to Total Profit

Measures each product's share of overall profit.

**Formula:**

`Profit Contribution % = Product Profit / Total Profit × 100`

### 6. Average Order Value (AOV)

Measures average net revenue per unique order.

**Formula:**

`AOV = Total Net Revenue / Distinct Order Count`

## Business Questions & Analysis

### Q1. Which products and categories generate the highest revenue and profit?

Analyzed revenue and profit at both product and category levels to distinguish high-sales products from products that generate stronger profit.

**Analysis includes:**
- Revenue by product
- Profit by product
- Revenue by category
- Profit by category

### Q2. How do discounts affect profitability at the product and category level?

Compared discount impact, revenue, total profit, and profit margin across products and categories.

**Business purpose:** Identify where discounting creates a substantial financial impact and where promotional policies may need to be reviewed.

### Q3. Are there products that sell a lot but contribute very little to profit?

Compared units sold with total profit to identify products that generate substantial sales volume but relatively weaker profit.

**Business purpose:** Prioritize margin reviews, cost optimization, and alternative promotional approaches for products that may be underperforming financially.

### Q4. Which products can have price adjustments without impacting sales volume significantly?

Evaluated product sales volume, profit margin, average discount percentage, and discount impact to shortlist candidates for controlled price-adjustment tests.

**Important limitation:** Historical price elasticity is not available in the dataset. The analysis identifies potential candidates; it does not establish that increasing prices will leave sales volume unchanged.

### Q5. What promotions or bundling strategies can improve overall profitability?

Developed recommendations based on product demand, margins, and discount behavior, including:

- Meal and beverage bundles
- High-margin product add-ons
- Targeted promotions instead of blanket discounts
- Controlled quantity-based offers
- Testing promotional structures against profitability measures

**Important limitation:** The dataset does not establish which products customers purchase together. Bundle recommendations are therefore based on product performance rather than observed co-purchase behavior.

## Key Findings

The overall results indicate that the dataset generated approximately **₹10.76 lakh in net revenue**, **₹4.84 lakh in profit**, and an overall profit margin of **45.0%**.

The product and category analyses are designed to distinguish revenue leaders from profit leaders, evaluate discount exposure, and identify candidates for pricing and promotional experiments.

The final recommendations focus on protecting margins, using targeted promotions, testing controlled price changes, and evaluating bundles using incremental profit rather than sales volume alone.

## Business Recommendations

1. **Optimize discounts:** Review discount-heavy products and avoid broad discounts where demand and profitability are already strong.
2. **Test product bundles:** Evaluate meal-and-beverage or product-and-add-on combinations using small, controlled bundle discounts.
3. **Protect high-volume margins:** Review product costs and promotional exposure for products that sell frequently but generate relatively weaker profit.
4. **Run controlled pricing experiments:** Test potential price changes against a control group and monitor units sold, revenue, profit margin, and repeat purchases.
5. **Measure incremental profitability:** Evaluate each promotion or bundle based on additional profit after discounts and costs, rather than revenue growth alone.

These are recommendations for testing and evaluation, not claims of realized business improvements.
